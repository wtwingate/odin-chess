** Notes

*** Board State

The most important decision to make at the beginning is what data
structure to use for the board. The chess board is represented as a grid
of 64 squares with ranks (rows) and files (columns) like so:

+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| h1 | h2 | h3 | h4 | h5 | h6 | h7 | h8 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| g1 | g2 | g3 | g4 | g5 | g6 | g7 | g8 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| f1 | f2 | f3 | f4 | f5 | f6 | f7 | f8 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| e1 | e2 | e3 | e4 | e5 | e6 | e7 | e8 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| d1 | d2 | d3 | d4 | d5 | d6 | d7 | d8 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| c1 | c2 | c3 | c4 | c5 | c6 | c7 | c8 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| b1 | b2 | b3 | b4 | b5 | b6 | b7 | b8 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| a1 | a2 | a3 | a4 | a5 | a6 | a7 | a8 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+

We could use a 2D array to represent the board so that we index into
each square with [rank][file], or we could place all 64 squares in a
single array and index into them like so:

+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| 56 | 57 | 58 | 59 | 60 | 61 | 62 | 63 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| 48 | 49 | 50 | 51 | 52 | 53 | 54 | 55 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| 40 | 41 | 42 | 43 | 44 | 45 | 46 | 47 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| 32 | 33 | 34 | 35 | 36 | 37 | 38 | 39 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| 24 | 25 | 26 | 27 | 28 | 29 | 30 | 31 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| 16 | 17 | 18 | 19 | 20 | 21 | 22 | 23 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| 08 | 09 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 | 15 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+
| 00 | 01 | 02 | 03 | 04 | 05 | 06 | 07 |
+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+

Using a single array for our squares allows us to iterate through all
the squares and calculate moves a bit more efficiently, at the cost of a
little mental overhead. The movement offsets are as follows, and can be
easily extended for pieces with "ray" attack types (e.g rook, bishop).

+----+----+----+
| +7 | +8 | +9 |
+----+----+----+
| -1 | XX | +1 |
+----+----+----+
| -9 | -8 | -7 |
+----+----+----+

*** Input & Notation

[[https://www.chessprogramming.org/Algebraic_Chess_Notation][Algebraic Chess Notation]]

Input will need to be able to parse standard Algebraic Notation in both
its short and long forms. It will need to be able to detect possible
ambiguities in the input and raise an appropriate error.

For our internal representation of the history, we'll use a subset
called Pure Coordinate Notation, perhaps as specified in the Universal
Chess Interface (UCI) protocol.
